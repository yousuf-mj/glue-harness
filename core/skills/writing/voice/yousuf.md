# Voice: Yousuf

Use for anything sent in my name. It applies in every tone; the tone file says where it bends.

Sources: a voice interview (2026-11-26) and my edits to meeting-invite drafts. Every line here is something I said or did. Add lines only with my yes.

## How it sounds
- A friendly colleague, not a manager making an announcement.
- Short and plain. If a line doesn't change what the reader does, cut it.
- Warm and specific, never gushing.

## Openings
- Slack: "Hey" with no name, or straight in with the point.
- Email or invite: "Hey [name]", "Hiya [name]" or "Morning [name]".

## Sign-offs
- Slack: none.
- Short Slack replies: often just an emoji (👍), or a short line such as "that's great thanks" or "yes go ahead".
- Email: "thanks" or "cheers" with no punctuation, then my name.

## Emojis
- As a whole reply or a reaction: yes.
- Inside a message I've written: no.

## Asking for something
- A soft question with the reason: "Hey, would you be able to look at this when you get a sec? It's blocking the release"
- Soft is fine. Apologetic is not: no "sorry to bother you", "just wanted to" or "I was wondering if maybe".

## Saying thanks
- Warm and specific: say what the help made possible. "Thanks for jumping on that so quickly, really helped us get the release out"

## Bad news
- Context first, then the news, then what it means for the reader.

## Structure
- Several points: one short intro line, then bullets.
- Meeting titles: names first, then the purpose. "Yousuf / [name] - Intro coffee chat"

## Words I use
- "no worries", "all good", "sounds good", "cool", "let me know if you need anything", "quick question"

## Words I never use
- Corporate: circle back, touch base, leverage, synergy, bandwidth
- Over-formal: Kind regards, Please find attached, I hope this email finds you well
- Filler and hedges: just wanted to, sorry to bother you, I was wondering if maybe

## Evidence from my edits
- Called this "way too formal" for a first catch-up invite: "I'm Yousuf, and I joined the team this week as the new Engineering Manager. I'd love to have an online coffee chat with you so we can get to know each other."
- Cut "There's no agenda and nothing to prepare" and "I'll share a bit about myself too" from an invite, and kept them out when offered again. Short beats reassuring.
